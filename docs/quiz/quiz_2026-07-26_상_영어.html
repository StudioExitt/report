주제 파악 (Finding the Main Idea) · 지문을 읽고 가장 적절한 주제를 고르세요.
유형: 영어 · 주제 찾기 난이도: 상

지문 Reading Passage

For much of the twentieth century, economists modeled willpower as a fixed personal trait, something a person simply had more or less of, rather like height or eye color. Self-control failures, from skipped workouts to broken diets, were treated as evidence of weak character. Over the past two decades, however, a body of research in behavioral psychology has converged on a different and considerably more mechanical picture: willpower behaves less like a trait and more like a depletable resource, one that is drawn down by every act of deliberate self-regulation over the course of a day and replenished only through rest.

The clearest demonstrations of this pattern come from laboratory studies in which participants are first asked to perform a task requiring sustained self-control, such as resisting a plate of fresh cookies in favor of a bowl of radishes, and are then given a second, seemingly unrelated task, such as persisting at an unsolvable puzzle. Participants who exercised restraint in the first task reliably gave up on the second task sooner than those who had not been asked to restrain themselves at all, even though the two tasks shared no obvious surface connection. Researchers labeled this carryover effect "ego depletion," and for a time it was treated as one of the more robust findings in social psychology.

What makes the phenomenon consequential well beyond the laboratory is that everyday self-regulation rarely announces itself as such. Deciding what to eat, choosing words carefully in a tense conversation, resisting the urge to check a phone during a meeting, and suppressing a yawn during a lecture all draw on the same limited pool, according to this model, even though none of these acts feels like "using willpower" in the way that resisting a slice of cake does. A person who has spent the morning making a long series of small, effortful decisions may therefore arrive at an important choice in the afternoon with less capacity for careful deliberation than the morning version of themselves, quite apart from how tired or hungry they consciously feel.

This resource-based account has not gone unchallenged. A large-scale replication effort published in the mid-2010s, involving dozens of laboratories testing thousands of participants under standardized conditions, found an effect size for ego depletion close to zero, reigniting a debate about whether the original studies had been distorted by small sample sizes and selective publication of positive results. Some researchers now argue that what looks like depletion is better explained by shifting motivation or attention rather than the exhaustion of any literal internal reserve; a person may simply choose to stop trying on a boring second task, not because their capacity has run out, but because they no longer see the effort as worthwhile.

Whichever interpretation eventually prevails, the practical implications converge on similar ground. Institutions from courtrooms to hospitals have begun scheduling their most consequential decisions, such as parole hearings or complex diagnoses, earlier in the day or immediately after a break, on the theory that decision quality is not constant across the hours in which a person is awake. Whether the underlying cause is a genuinely depleting resource, a wandering motivation, or some combination the field has not yet cleanly separated, the broader lesson is the same: the conditions surrounding a decision, and not only the intelligence or character of the person making it, shape how well that decision is likely to be made.

문제 Question

What is the main idea of the passage?

  1. Willpower is a fixed personality trait that some people possess in greater amounts than others from birth.
  2. Research on whether self-control draws on a limited, depletable resource remains contested, but it has nonetheless reshaped how decision quality is understood as dependent on surrounding conditions rather than character alone.
  3. The 2010s replication crisis definitively proved that ego depletion does not exist and that self-control failures are always caused by low motivation.
  4. Courtrooms and hospitals should always schedule important decisions in the morning because afternoon decisions are provably worse.
  5. Resisting cookies in a laboratory experiment has no relevance to real-world decision-making outside controlled settings.
